the invention

The witness protocol.

Oxagen replaced the judge with an oracle. A task is done only when a witness test proves it — failing on the old code, passing on the new. Not an opinion. A flip.

1An independent verifier authors the witness in a shadow sandbox.
A separate model, in a detached workspace, after reading the executed diff. The worker never touches it, and the working tree is never mutated.
2The test runs on the old code. must fail
Proof the behavior was genuinely absent before the change.
3The test runs on the new code. must pass
Proof the behavior is genuinely present after it.
4Tamper evidence voids the flip.
If the worker modified the witness files, the proof is invalidated. No credit. And the flip is additional evidence, never a substitute: the project's own test suite still gates the change.

A deterministic system controls the definition of done. Models do the work — they never grade it. Failure modes, named and guarded: appendix A2.

appendix · A2 · for technical diligence

What the flip does not prove.

1A flip can be shallow.
A weak witness proves something changed, not that the right thing was delivered. Guards today: the witness is authored by an independent verifier model after reading the executed diff — never by the worker — and tampering voids credit. Roadmap: mutation-scored witness quality and adversarial witness review.
2A flip is silent about regressions.
It proves the named behavior arrived, not that nothing else broke. The project's own test suite still gates the change; the flip is additional evidence, never a substitute.
3Not all work has an oracle.
UI, refactors, performance, migrations, and design have no clean fail → pass test. We have not yet published a measured oracle-able share of enterprise work — we measure it per pilot, and non-oracle work runs under the warrant system: typed, pre-action justification instead of a flip. The corpus bias toward testable work is real; the training curriculum weights against it.
4Flips are optimizable.
A model trained on flips could learn to produce trivially-flippable work. Guards: the witness is written against the executed diff by a model with no stake in the reward; the worker never sees it in advance; voided flips are excluded from training; and the ladder abstains rather than credit an unprovable claim — an abstention is never training data.

A protocol with no named failure modes is an unexamined protocol. These are ours — with the guards, and the gaps.
