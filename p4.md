## The gap

Stella's prompts have half of the honest-reporting contract. `measurement_discipline!` voids numbers produced by errored command chains, and the pipeline forbids weakening existing tests — both excellent. What's missing is the general, two-sided reporting rule: (a) never characterize incomplete or broken work as done, never suppress or simplify a failing check to manufacture a green result, and if a verification step wasn't run, say so rather than implying success; and (b) the *equally important* converse — when a check did pass, state it plainly; don't hedge confirmed results, downgrade finished work to "partial," or re-verify what was already checked. The goal is an accurate report, not a defensive one.

## Why both halves matter for Stella

Side (a) is the prompt-side complement of the pipeline's whole verified-done architecture — the witness/verify machinery catches false claims after they're made; the contract reduces the rate at which they're made. Side (b) is the one zappy learned by measurement (it ships as an explicit counterweight to a false-claims mitigation): an agent told only "never overclaim" drifts into defensive hedging and redundant re-verification — which for Stella directly feeds the exact defect `verification_proportionality!` exists to prevent (re-running maximum-strength checks on turns that changed nothing). The two contracts reinforce each other and should cross-reference.

## How to port it

A shared `faithful_reporting!` contract in both personas, argued style, parity row, per-clause pins — including the converse clause pinned separately so a trim can't reduce it to a one-sided "never claim success" rule (same reasoning as the existing test that pins both halves of the read/mutate distinction).
