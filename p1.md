## The gap

Neither `SYSTEM_PROMPT` nor `PIPELINE_SYSTEM_PROMPT` (`crates/stella-cli/src/agent/prompt.rs:110`, `:142`) says anything about irreversibility, blast radius, or shared state. The closest thing is one clause inside `verification_proportionality!` ("Never reset working state to 'pristine'…"), which is scoped to verification. Nothing tells the model to treat `rm -rf`, force-push, dropping tables, or posting to external services differently from editing a local file; nothing says "do not bypass safety checks (`--no-verify`) to make an obstacle go away"; nothing says "investigate unexpected state before deleting it — it may be the user's in-progress work"; and nothing defines what a tool denial *means* (a policy refusal today is indistinguishable from an unknown tool, and the model gets no guidance not to re-attempt the identical call).

For an agent that runs pipelines autonomously, this is the largest content gap in the prompt.

## What the comparator carries

Zappy's "Executing actions with care" section is the strongest single section in its prompt: a reversibility/blast-radius frame (local+reversible = free; hard-to-reverse / shared-state / outward-facing = confirm first), concrete example classes (destructive ops, force-push/reset --hard, actions visible to others, uploading to third-party services publishes it), "one approval is not standing authorization — scope stands as specified," and the obstacle rule: fix root causes rather than bypassing safety checks; investigate locks/unfamiliar branches before deleting.

## How to port it (Stella's idiom, not zappy's)

Do NOT port the prose style — port the content as a fifth shared contract macro (`action_care!`), written the way the existing four are: argued, with the failure mode it prevents, embedded verbatim in both personas, with its row in `prompt/parity.rs` and per-clause pins so a trim can't hollow it out (the same discipline as `both_prompts_size_verification_to_what_the_turn_changed`). Include the denial semantics clause ("a refused tool call is an instruction to change approach, not to retry the same call"), which also papers over the current RequireApproval dead-end (#2676) until that's fixed. `ask_user` is the escalation surface the contract should name.
